## 0. Executive summary

The repository is unusually honest and well-engineered for an instrument-development stage: 209 tests pass (55 skipped without PyTorch), the renderer and rule semantics are clear, the documentation repeatedly refuses to overclaim, and the outputs preserve the exact caveats that a reviewer would otherwise have to reconstruct. That is a real strength.

The research design, however, has three blocking problems and several serious validity problems.

1. **P0 — Relation predicates are near-tautologies, so the relational contrast is confounded.** With existential semantics over three objects, `left_of(a,b)` is satisfied whenever any two objects have different x-coordinates; `above(a,b)` whenever any two have different y-coordinates. In the current 3×3 grid renderer the analytic singleton exposure is `0.999271` (`0.99930` simulated), and the saved singleton matrix reports min `0.99`, median `1.00` over `3,249` measurements. A relation singleton therefore cannot distinguish relational competence from arbitrary placement. Relation count is also confounded with object count, attribute count, rule length, endpoint binding, and template identity. H1's "more so as `K_rel` rises" and H2's "larger slope for relational rules" are not identified.

2. **P0 — The sampled exposure dose is one-sided and narrow, and the sampler does not reach the feasible dose.** Across the 20 sampled singleton-preserving designs, every observed rule lift is ≤ 0; the per-cell means range from 0.00 (`attr1_rel0`) to −1.63 (`attr3_rel2`). The median per-rule lift range is only `0.0955` log units (max `0.63`), and `98.7%` of the lift variance is between-rule. The exact LP polytope is wider (`−4.888` to `+0.337`, median width `1.172`, `23/57` rules admit positive lift), but the positive region is an extreme corner (`47/57` weights at the floor) that no sampled chain reaches. The design therefore still cannot support a two-sided dose-response, and the relational interaction is mostly extrapolation.

3. **P0 — There is no learnable model yet, so no outcome can be measured.** The documented 32,000-update pilot (width 16, batch 2) yielded only two inspected samples: a red shape for the red condition and no clear requested relation. The 12,000-update samples were noise. The proposal's Stage 0 gate does not include a learnability criterion, and the Stage 1 calibration target (median reference RSR in [0.3, 0.7]) has never been approached. Until a model reaches nontrivial RSR, every downstream RSR, `N50`, and `Γ` number is uninterpretable.

4. **P1 — The decorrelation gate fails by the proposal's own stated criterion.** `outputs/design-diagnostics-57.json` reports pooled `r(log lift, log marginal) = −0.446` and VIF for log lift of **11.61**, against the proposal's gate of `|r| ≤ 0.3` and `VIF < 2`. The repository labels this `not_assessed` because the analysis is exploratory, but the proposal's gate is explicit. Either the gate is revised to the correct (within-rule) level, or Stage 0 is not passed.

5. **P1 — The pooled correlation is a between-rule artifact.** On the holdout matrix, the pooled correlation is −0.464 while the within-rule correlation is **−0.00092**. Rules differ in mean lift, so pooling creates a spurious marginal-lift relationship. Within a rule the marginal is fixed at machine precision (range `2.2e−16`), so `β_m` is **exactly collinear** with the rule intercept. The gate should be evaluated on within-rule variation or on the conditioning of the actual design matrix, not on pooled rows.

6. **P1 — The template set is not counterbalanced.** The 57 templates are a hand-designed bundle set, not a factorial design. Only **9 of 12** color–shape pairs occur, with counts from 3 to 9, and only **3 of 12** are co-bound to the same object. Relation kind is assigned deterministically by bundle index: in `K_attr = 2, K_rel = 1`, `left_of` is subject-only `3/3` and `above` is both-bound `3/3`. `load_rules` now rejects duplicate and contradictory predicates (fixed), but the design itself remains confounded.

7. **P1 — The checker is effectively an inverse renderer.** It hard-codes `count == 3`, bounding-box width `12`, height `11` or `12`, and tight fill-ratio ranges. It is validated on clean and perturbed renders, not on generated images. At blur radius 1.0 it rejects `169/200` (`84.5%`) and `90/100` (`90%`) of inputs; among accepted images there are zero false accepts and zero false rejects, so the errors are **attrition**, not misclassification. Conditioning on accepted images changes the estimand to a selected subpopulation. The human audit is correctly designed but blocked on suitable images.

8. **P1 — The A-matrix and design optimization do not propagate uncertainty.** At 100 scenes/condition, the Monte Carlo standard error of `log n_r` at `n_r = 0.05` is about **0.44**; at 10^5 scenes it is about **0.014**. The saved matrix is 100 scenes/condition. The LP treats the estimated matrix as exact, and the min-KL objective is not the research objective (it does not maximize information about `β_λ` or `β_λR`). The SLSQP refinement starts from the LP vertex, exactly where the KL gradient is worst.

9. **P1 — The statistical model is over-parameterized for the variation that exists.** Within a rule, the design fixes the singleton marginals exactly, so `log m̄` has zero within-rule variation and `β_m` is collinear with the rule intercept. The template random intercept absorbs the rule-level mean, leaving `β_λ` identified from a `0.0955` log-unit realized range. `β_λR` is a cross-level interaction with even less support. The plan needs a measurement-error / hierarchical model and a design that targets the dose spread.

The most valuable adjacent work is **MOSAIC** (Jeong et al., ICML 2026), which already studies multi-object spatial relations, attribute, counting, complexity versus imbalance, and compositional holdouts in a controlled synthetic framework. The differentiator for this project cannot be "a synthetic compositional benchmark"; it must be the **exposure-level decomposition** (predicate vs pair vs rule), the **marginal-preserving manipulation**, the **dose-response / N50 framing**, and the **allocation** contribution. The proposal's own "lift" terminology also collides with Yu & Gao's inference-time "lift scores"; rename it.

The rest of this document gives the evidence, the code-level review, the literature positioning, and a prioritized path forward.

## 1. What the repository gets right

These are not throwaway compliments; they are the parts to preserve while fixing the science.

- **Clear rule semantics.** `exposure/rules.py` implements existential quantification over distinct object bindings, with shared variable names retained within a rule or subset. The docstring is precise about ties and image coordinates. The `satisfies` implementation enumerates permutations and is obviously correct for the prototype's scale.
- **Honest documentation.** `docs/stage0-status.md` says "**Not passed**" in the first line and distinguishes instrument-development results from confirmatory findings. `docs/architecture.md` lists current limits. `docs/design-strata.md` explicitly warns that within-rule correlation can be undefined and that rounding noise can produce spurious correlations. This is better scientific hygiene than most research repositories.
- **Good test discipline for a prototype.** 209 tests pass; the suite covers rules, rendering, measurement, mixtures, design feasibility, diagnostics, checker perturbations, human-audit plumbing, and Gaussian diagnostics. The 55 skips are all PyTorch-dependent, not silent passes.
- **Determinism and provenance.** `pilot_train.py` records config, RNG state, runtime, peak memory, condition counts, and realized co-occurrence counts in checkpoints. The resume path checks compatibility. This is the right foundation for reproducible runs.
- **No fabricated human labels.** `human_audit.py` and `audit_agreement.py` prepare blinded reviews and preserve disagreements; they explicitly refuse to create labels. The proposal's human-audit requirement is treated seriously.
- **The Gaussian diagnostic is a good instinct.** `gaussian_diagnostic.py` validates loss normalization against an analytic Bayes denoiser. It is only one-dimensional, but the idea of an exact reference is exactly what the project needs more of.
- **The design-subspace and strata analyses are honest about their limits.** They do not claim the proposal's gate is passed. `design-strata.md` documents the pooled-versus-within-rule distinction, which is the right diagnostic instinct even if the current analysis stops short.

The engineering is not the problem. The problem is that the estimand, the benchmark, and the model are not yet in a state where the estimand can be measured.

## 2. P0 issues (including one retracted claim)

### 2.1 The composition gap: an invalid criticism, and the specification issue that remains

> **Correction (2026-11-05).** This section originally claimed that the
> proposal's `Γ = logit(RSR) − logit(Π c_p)` is negative by construction under
> the multiplicative null. That claim was wrong. Because `logit` is injective,
> `logit(RSR) − logit(Π c_p) = 0` whenever `RSR = Π c_p` in the interior of the
> probability domain. The original table belongs to
> `logit(Π c_p) − Σ logit(c_p)`, a different quantity. The corrected derivation
> and a regression test live in `exposure/composition.py` and
> `tests/test_composition.py`.

**The definition.** The proposal (Section 4.2) defines, for a rule `r` with predicate set `P_r` and singleton rule-satisfaction rates `c_p`:

```
ĉ_r = Π_{p ∈ P_r} c_p
Γ_r = logit(RSR_r) − logit(ĉ_r)
```

**The null.** Okawa et al. (NeurIPS 2023) report that compositional performance depends multiplicatively on constituent performance. If the intended null is `RSR_r = Π_p c_p`, then both scales are zero under that null:

```
Γ_r^logit = logit(RSR_r) − logit(Π_p c_p) = 0
Γ_r^log   = log RSR_r − Σ_p log c_p      = 0
```

The two scales are different transformations of the same deviation, not competing nulls. `Γ^logit` changes sign when `RSR` and `Π c_p` are swapped and is unbounded near probability boundaries; `Γ^log` is additive over predicates and unbounded below. Neither is "wrong", but they answer different questions and must not be mixed.

**What the original table actually computed.** For equal `c` and `k` predicates, the review evaluated

```
logit(c^k) − k·logit(c) = −2.20 (c=0.8,k=2), −4.11 (c=0.8,k=3), ...
```

That expression is not `Γ^logit`; it subtracts the sum of logits rather than the logit of the product. It is a measure of how much `Σ logit(c_p)` overstates `logit(Π c_p)`. It is large and negative by construction, but it says nothing about the proposal's stated estimand. The map from `Γ^logit` to `K_rel` is not an arithmetic identity.

**The specification issue that does survive.** Even the log-scale product null is a heuristic: when predicates share object variables, the product of singleton marginals is not a coherent joint distribution. For example, `red(a) ∧ circle(a)` requires the *same* object to be red and circular, while `c_red · c_circle` is the probability that some object is red times the probability that some object is circular, which can be satisfied by different objects. The product null therefore penalizes binding even when the model has learned the data distribution. A principled null is a **log-linear (maximum-entropy) model** over the predicate indicators that matches all lower-order marginals (singletons, and optionally pairs) but has no higher-order interaction. The composition gap is then the highest-order interaction term, or the KL divergence between the empirical conditional and the maximum-entropy conditional. This handles binding, relations, and shared variables in one framework.

Two further scale issues remain real but are not algebra defects:

- **Boundary behavior.** Empirical rates of exactly `0` or `1` make `logit` infinite and `log` undefined. The implementation must return an explicit status, not an infinity; `exposure.composition.composition_gaps` does this and reports `gamma_logit`, `gamma_log`, and `gamma_status` for every rule and pair.
- **Scale choice is a pre-registration decision.** `Γ^logit` and `Γ^log` can rank rules differently. The proposal should name one as primary, justify it in outcome units, and report the other as a sensitivity.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Pre-register `Γ^log = log RSR_r − Σ log c_p` as the primary estimand, with the corrected `Γ^logit` as a sensitivity. Add a null simulation that verifies both are zero under `RSR = Π c_p`; the repository now has this check in `tests/test_composition.py`.
2. Better, pre-register a **binding-aware log-linear null**: fit a maximum-entropy distribution over predicate indicators with all lower-order terms, and define the gap as the residual high-order interaction (or the KL to the fitted null). This is the generalization of "multiplicative" that survives shared variables.
3. Report boundary status (`defined`, `log_only`, `undefined`) rather than dropping saturated rules silently.
4. Do not use `Γ` as the sole primary outcome. Pair it with `N50` (which is a dose, not a probability) and with a direct contrast between the model's conditional and the data conditional.

### 2.2 The sampled dose is one-sided and narrow; the feasible set is wider

> **Correction (2026-11-05).** Independent verification re-scoped this concern.
> The 20-design *sample* is one-sided and narrow, but the exact linear-program
> feasible set is not. Over the singleton-preserving polytope, lift ranges from
> `−4.888` to `+0.337`, median width `1.172`, and `23/57` rules admit strictly
> positive lift. The positive-lift region is an extreme corner: a 5-chain
> hit-and-run run cannot reach it, and the attained corner has `47/57` weights
> at the floor. The correct statement is therefore "the current sampler does
> not explore the feasible dose," not "the instrument cannot express positive
> lift."

**Observed lifts.** Using the saved 57-condition pilot matrix (`outputs/exposure-57-pilot.json`) and the 20 sampled designs (`outputs/design-diagnostics-57.json`), the rule-level `log_lift` values are:

- all attribute-rule lifts are ≤ 0 (max 0.0, min −0.546 in the uniform mixture);
- across the 20 designs, the maximum lift for any rule is 0.0;
- per-cell means range from 0.00 (`attr1_rel0`) to −1.63 (`attr3_rel2`).

These are properties of the sampled designs, not of the feasible set.

Two things follow about the *sample*. First, the realized dose is a **deficit**: no sampled design puts a rule above its independence baseline. Second, the **within-rule range** is tiny: across the 20 designs the median per-rule range `max(log_lift) − min(log_lift)` is `0.0955` log units (min `0.000`, max `0.631`), while adjacent cell medians differ by roughly `0.3–0.6` log units. Longer hit-and-run runs improve the realized range only modestly (5 chains × 200 designs give `0.148–0.182`), so this must be diagnosed with exact LP bounds and ESS/R-hat rather than assumed to be a mixing artifact alone.

**Why the sample stays negative.** In the current renderer, attributes are sampled independently, and conditioning on a template does not correlate the attributes of *other* objects. A mixture of independent components can induce positive correlation in principle, and the exact LP confirms that it can here, but only in a corner of the polytope where almost every weight sits at its floor. The uniform-mixture diagonal for single-attribute rules is exactly zero by construction (`n_r = c_r`), and multi-predicate rules are negative because of binding. Hit-and-run with `burn_in=1000` and `thin=10` remains in the negative region; this is a mixing/design-coverage failure, not proof that the instrument lacks a positive-lift arm.

**Why this still threatens H2.** H2 says "at fixed predicate marginals, `Γ` increases with lift `λ`." If the *realized* `λ` only ranges over [−1.8, 0] and within-rule variation is ~0.1, then `β_λ` is identified from a short, possibly nonlinear segment, and `β_λR` is identified mainly by between-cell differences in the *level* of `λ`, which are confounded with `K_rel`, `K_attr`, object count, and template identity. The exact bounds show a wider set exists; they do not show the current sampler reaches it. The slope may be real, but the current design cannot separate it from the cell effects.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Build a factorial template set** (shape × color × relation × binding) rather than 57 hand-designed bundles. The full crossing gives a known, balanced design and makes lift a continuous function of the weights.
2. **Add coupled templates** that deliberately raise co-occurrence above independence (e.g., a component in which red objects are always circles, or a relation is only rendered between specific attribute pairs). This gives positive-lift points and turns the dose into a two-sided contrast.
3. **Target the coefficients, not closeness to uniform.** Use a D-optimal or c-optimal design for `β_λ` and `β_λR`, or directly maximize the minimum within-rule range subject to the marginal constraints. Report the exact LP bounds alongside every sample so a narrow sample is never mistaken for a narrow polytope.
4. **Report the attainable range per rule** and drop rules whose range is below a pre-registered threshold (e.g., 0.5 log units) from the slope analysis. This is more honest than fitting a slope to noise.
5. **Use exact vertex enumeration or a properly converged multi-chain hit-and-run** with ESS/R-hat diagnostics. `exposure.design_bounds` now computes the exact LP bounds and `exposure.design_chains` now reports split-R-hat and autocorrelation ESS; the current default sampler still lacks them.

### 2.3 Relations are near-tautologies

**The semantics.** `satisfies` existentially quantifies over distinct object variables. A rule containing only `left_of(a,b)` is satisfied if there exist two distinct objects with `x_a < x_b`. With three objects on a 3×3 grid with jitter, the probability that all x-coordinates are equal is `(3/C(9,3))·(1/49) = 1/1372`, so the analytic singleton exposure is `0.999271`; a 200,000-scene simulation gives `0.99930` for `left_of` and `0.99916` for `above`. The saved singleton matrix reports relation exposure min `0.99`, median `1.00` across `3,249` measurements.

**Consequences.**

- Relation singleton `c_p` is near 1. Under `Γ^logit` this makes the logit large, so the *sensitivity* scale is ill-conditioned, but it does **not** create a nonzero gap under the multiplicative null. The real problem is benchmark validity: a relation singleton cannot distinguish relational competence from arbitrary object placement.
- Relation exposure is not manipulable at the marginal level: you cannot meaningfully vary the probability of "some object is left of some other object" when it is already `≈0.999`. The `K_rel` manipulation therefore changes rule length and object count, not relation exposure.
- The "relational complexity" contrast is confounded with the number of objects and the number of attributes. A 2-relation chain needs three objects; a 0-relation 3-attribute rule also needs up to three objects. The current cell table does not separate these.
- The saturation also hides a template confound: relation kind is assigned deterministically by bundle index, so in `K_attr=2, K_rel=1` every `left_of` template binds only the subject (`3/3`) while every `above` template binds both endpoints (`3/3`). See §3.1.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Bind relations to attributed objects**: `red(a) ∧ left_of(a,b) ∧ square(b)` rather than `red(a) ∧ left_of(a,b)`. Then relation exposure is conditional on the attribute binding and can be far from 1.
2. **Add graded relations**: distance thresholds (`left_of` by at least `d` pixels), angular relations, or "near/far" predicates. This makes relation exposure continuous and manipulable.
3. **Add negative relations** (`not left_of`) or use a larger object count so that the existential relation is not trivially satisfied.
4. **Report relation exposure conditional on the attribute bundle**, not the marginal. The current `design-subspace` singleton events are marginal and therefore hide the tautology.
5. **Redesign the cell table** so that `K_rel` is not confounded with object count. Use a factorial `(objects, attributes, relations)` design and report the object-count covariate explicitly.

### 2.4 There is no learnable model yet

**The evidence.**

- `docs/stage0-status.md` and `docs/pilot-training.md`: at 12,000 updates the online velocity samples were noise; at 32,000 updates only two visual samples were inspected (a red condition showed a red shape; the relational sample did not clearly satisfy the requested relation); the fixed-scene diagnostic MSE was 0.8017 on [0,1]. These are inspection counts, not a quality score.
- `pilot_train.py`: width 16, batch 2, 1,000 timesteps, 1e-4 AdamW, no LR schedule, no gradient clipping, no x0 clipping during sampling, one cross-attention block at 16×16.
- The proposal's Stage 1 calibration target is median reference RSR in [0.3, 0.7]. Nothing in the repository is close.
- The current Stage 0 gate (checker error, design decorrelation, MOSAIC direction) has no model criterion at all, so it is possible to "pass" with no usable model.

**Why this blocks everything.** `RSR`, `N50`, `Γ`, and the allocation surrogate are all functions of a trained model's behavior. If the model cannot generate three objects reliably, then RSR is near zero for all rules, the composition gap is dominated by floor effects, and the exposure design cannot be evaluated. The current Stage 0 gate (checker error, design decorrelation, MOSAIC direction) does not include a learnability criterion, so it is possible to "pass" Stage 0 with no usable model.

**Architecture and training gaps.**

- **Capacity.** Width 16 is tiny for 64×64 three-object scenes. The model has one 16×16 cross-attention block and no explicit 2D positional encoding. Relations need spatial precision that a single coarse attention map may not provide.
- **Data.** 32,000 updates × batch 2 = 64,000 scenes, about 1,100 scenes per template. This is far below what is needed for a from-scratch conditional diffusion model to learn three-object compositions.
- **Optimization.** No LR warmup or cosine schedule, no gradient clipping, no Min-SNR weighting, default AdamW betas. The loss is plain epsilon-MSE.
- **Sampling.** The DDPM loop updates `x` but never clips the implied clean sample / `x0` estimate to [−1, 1] at intermediate steps; only the final image is clamped. This is a known source of drift and color saturation in small models.
- **Conditioning.** The null condition is an all-padding mask replaced by a learned null token, which is fine, but the structured scene-graph encoder is only partially implemented: predicate tokens have subject/object embeddings, but there are no object-slot attention layers or explicit binding across predicates.
- **Reference model.** There is no reference model trained on uniform π at the same budget, so "excess loss" cannot be computed. The Gaussian diagnostic is 1D and does not exercise the image model.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Add a learnability pre-gate to Stage 0** (or a Stage 0.5):
   - overfit one fixed scene: MSE < 0.01 and visually correct;
   - overfit 10 scenes: RSR ≥ 0.9 on those 10;
   - overfit 100 scenes: RSR ≥ 0.7;
   - full 57-template mixture: median RSR ≥ 0.3 with a capacity/budget sweep.
   If the pre-gate fails, do not run exposure experiments. Report the learnability failure as the result.
2. **Increase capacity and compute**: width 64–128, batch 32–64, 100k–1M updates, 2D positional encoding, 2–4 cross-attention blocks, object-slot attention, and a cosine LR schedule with warmup and gradient clipping.
3. **Add x0 clipping / dynamic thresholding** at each sampling step.
4. **Use a curriculum**: one object → two objects → three objects; attribute-only → single relation → chains. This is also a scientific manipulation (complexity), so document it as such.
5. **Revise the compute estimate.** The current 200–250 GPU-hour estimate assumes 3 GPU-h/run at width 16/batch 2. A model that actually learns may need 10–100× more. Measure tokens/scene throughput and update the estimate before committing to 60 runs.
6. **Build an exact or high-accuracy reference** for the synthetic distribution: enumerate the finite scene space (3×3 grid, 3 objects, 4 colors, 3 shapes) and compute the Bayes-optimal denoiser loss and conditional distributions. This gives a principled reference for “excess loss” and separates model failure from data geometry.

## 3. P1 instrument and benchmark problems

### 3.1 The template set is not counterbalanced

`exposure/templates.py` is explicit that the 57 templates are "a provisional, hand-designed set, not a counterbalanced or exhaustive benchmark." That is honest, but the proposal's H3/H4 and the `K_attr`/`K_rel` contrasts assume counterbalancing. The actual set has structural confounds:

- **Color–shape coverage is incomplete and imbalanced.** Only 9 of 12 pairs occur. Counts: blue–square 9, green–triangle 9, red–circle 9, red–square 6, yellow–circle 6, blue–circle 3, blue–triangle 3, green–square 3, yellow–triangle 3. The pairs red–triangle, green–circle, and yellow–square never occur.
- **Relation kind is confounded with attribute type and endpoint binding.** The assignment is deterministic by bundle index, not counterbalanced. In the `K_attr = 1` cells, `left_of` is paired with red/blue/green colors, while `above` is paired with yellow plus shapes. In `K_attr = 2, K_rel = 1`, every `left_of` template binds only the subject (`3/3`) and every `above` template binds both endpoints (`3/3`). A relation effect is therefore also an attribute-bundle and binding effect. Verified by `exposure/benchmark_audit.py`.
- **Same-object color–shape coverage is only 3/12.** Nine of twelve pairs occur *somewhere*, but only `red/circle`, `blue/square`, and `green/triangle` are co-bound to the same object. The other six pairs can only be satisfied by different objects, so a "color × shape" contrast does not exist for them.
- **The cell sizes differ**: 7 templates in each `K_attr = 1` cell, 6 in the others. The extra template is a triangle candidate needed to cover the shape vocabulary; it is not a balanced design.
- **No held-out split.** `docs/templates.md` says there is no held-out set, yet H3 requires one. The current `design-holdout` is a measurement holdout (different seeds), not a template holdout.
- **Duplicate and contradictory predicates are now rejected.** `exposure.rules.validate_rule` (called by `render.parse_rule` and therefore `measure.load_rules`) rejects duplicate identical predicates, two values for the same attribute on the same variable, and cyclic strict relations. This fixes the loader gap identified in the original review; fixtures that relied on permissive behavior were updated.

**Recommended fix.**

1. Replace the hand-designed bundle set with a **factorial template generator**: `(object_count, attribute_count, relation_count, relation_kind, binding_pattern)` fully crossed, with a fixed number of templates per cell and a seeded, balanced choice of concrete colors/shapes.
2. **Audit balance on actual predicate frequencies**, not vocabulary presence. `template_balance.py` currently counts whether a color/shape appears in a cell, not how often it co-occurs with other predicates. Add a contingency table of color × shape × relation × cell and report chi-square/standardized residuals. `benchmark_audit.py` now reports the co-binding gap and a machine-readable `confirmatory_ready` flag (currently false).
3. **Add a held-out template split** stratified by cell, and use it for H3 and for the allocation validation.
4. **Reject duplicate and contradictory predicates** in `load_rules` — **implemented** in `exposure/rules.validate_rule`. At minimum, warn and deduplicate; the loader now rejects.
5. **Report the realized exposure matrix for the held-out split**; a template holdout that is not exposure-matched is not a clean test.

### 3.2 The checker is an inverse renderer

`exposure/checker.py` hard-codes:

- `count == 3` connected components;
- bounding-box `width == 12` and `height in (11, 12)`;
- fill-ratio windows `[0.49, 0.56]`, `[0.76, 0.84]`, or `>= 0.97`;
- color purity `>= 0.95`;
- exact palette and background detection.

This is essentially the inverse of the renderer. It is appropriate for auditing clean renders, but it is not a valid checker for generated images, which will have blur, merged components, color drift, and variable object sizes. The perturbation audit confirms the fragility: at brightness 1.10 and blur radius 0.5, coverage drops to 114/200; at blur radius 1.0, to 31/200 (rejection 169/200, 84.5%); on a separate 100-scene holdout, blur radius 1.0 rejects 90/100. Among accepted images there are `0` false accepts and `0` false rejects, so the reported errors are almost entirely **attrition**: the checker abstains rather than misclassifying. That distinction matters because conditioning the analysis on accepted images changes the estimand to a selected subpopulation. The human audit is blocked because there are no suitable generated images.

**Per-rule metrics.** `exposure.checker.audit` and `exposure.checker_perturb.audit` now report rejection rate, coverage, conditional sensitivity/specificity (accepted images only), and unconditional sensitivity/specificity (detector failures counted as missed positives) for every rule and perturbation. These metrics are necessary but do not substitute for validation on generated images.

**Why this matters for the science.** The proposal's Stage 0 gate is checker error ≤ 2% on real renders and a human-audited sample of generated images. If the checker's false-reject rate differs by rule (e.g., relational rules produce touching objects, or rare colors are harder to segment), then RSR differences across rules are partly checker artifacts. The proposal acknowledges this risk but the current instrument does not measure it.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Replace the geometric checker with a robust, learned, or model-based checker** validated on generated images. A small segmentation model trained on renderer masks plus augmentations is a reasonable start; a classical pipeline with tolerant component merging, color clustering, and size-adaptive geometry is another.
2. **Report per-predicate and per-rule sensitivity/specificity**, not just overall coverage. The current audit reports accepted/rejected counts, not confusion matrices against ground truth on generated images.
3. **Use a latent-class / measurement-error model** to correct RSR for checker error. If the checker has known per-rule false-positive and false-negative rates, the observed RSR can be deconvolved; this is especially important for rare rules.
4. **Do not hard-code object count and bounding-box dimensions.** Use the renderer's annotation distribution to set tolerances, and test on out-of-distribution generated samples.
5. **Keep the human audit blinded and pre-register the sampling frame.** The existing tooling is good; the missing piece is images and a pre-registered rule-level sample size.

### 3.3 A-matrix precision and design optimization

**Monte Carlo precision.** `measure.py` estimates each A-matrix entry from `samples` accepted scenes per condition. At `n = 0.05` and 100 samples, the binomial standard error is `sqrt(0.05·0.95/100) = 0.0218`; on the log scale, `SE(log n) ≈ 0.0218/0.05 = 0.44`. At 10^5 samples, `SE(log n) ≈ 0.014`. The saved 57-condition pilot uses 100 samples/condition. The proposal calls for ~10^5 scenes/template: three orders of magnitude more samples, and about 32× smaller log-scale standard error.

**Runtime.** The pure-Python estimator processes roughly 0.5–2.5k scenes/s depending on whether subset measurements are enabled; the saved 57-condition pilot uses subsets and runs at about 0.6k scenes/s on this machine. 57 templates × 10^5 scenes = 5.7M scenes ≈ 1–3 hours on one core; 10^4 scenes/template ≈ 4–16 minutes. Vectorizing the renderer or evaluating annotations directly (without rasterizing) would make 10^5/template comfortably practical.

**Uncertainty propagation.** `design.py` solves the LP on the plug-in matrix and reports feasibility/optimality as if the entries were exact. A constraint that is feasible at the point estimate may be infeasible under sampling error, and a min-KL solution may be dominated by noise. The proposal's own caveat says "uncertainty is not propagated," but this is a P1 issue for the design gate.

**Objective mismatch.** Min-KL to uniform is a reasonable way to pick a "central" design, but it is not the design that maximizes information about `β_λ` or `β_λR`. The latter is a D-optimal or c-optimal design problem: maximize `log det(X^T W X)` or minimize the variance of the target contrast, where `X` includes the rule-level lift and its `K_rel` interaction. The current approach may produce designs that look decorrelated but have poor power for the primary estimand.

**SLSQP initialization.** `design.py` first solves a feasibility LP, then passes `solution.x` (a vertex) to SLSQP for the KL objective. The KL gradient is `log(count·w) + 1`, which is unbounded at zero; the code floors at 1e-15. Starting at a vertex with many zero weights is a bad initialization. A convex solver for the KL problem (exponential cone / ECOS / SCS) or an interior-point start would be more reliable.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Vectorize the A-matrix** and use at least 10^4–10^5 scenes/template. Evaluate annotations directly where possible; the rendering is only needed for the checker/visual audit.
2. **Propagate uncertainty** via bootstrap or a Bayesian posterior over the matrix. Solve a **chance-constrained** or **robust** LP (e.g., require constraints to hold with probability 1−α), and report the feasible region under uncertainty.
3. **Replace min-KL with a design objective** tied to the primary estimand: D-optimal for the `(λ, λ×K_rel, log m̄, K_attr, K_rel)` model, or c-optimal for `β_λ` and `β_λR`. Report both the min-KL and D-optimal designs for comparison.
4. **Use a convex KL solver** or start SLSQP from an interior point (e.g., the analytic center or a slightly smoothed uniform). Check KKT residuals.
5. **Add convergence diagnostics** to hit-and-run: multiple chains, R-hat, ESS, autocorrelation, and a trace plot. The current `design_sample.py` only checks numerical feasibility.

### 3.4 Design diagnostics are at the wrong level

`outputs/design-diagnostics-57.json` reports pooled correlations over 1,140 rule-by-design rows:

- `r(log_mean_marginal, log_lift) = −0.446`;
- VIF for `log_lift = 11.61`, for `k_rel = 13.72`;
- `gate_status: not_assessed`.

By the proposal's stated gate (`|r| ≤ 0.3`, `VIF < 2`), this fails. The repository's caution is appropriate, but the proposal's gate is not met.

The deeper issue is aggregation. `outputs/design-strata-holdout-76001.json` reports pooled −0.464 but **within-rule −0.00092**. The pooled correlation is a between-rule artifact: rules with lower mean lift also have lower mean marginal exposure, so pooling creates a negative relationship that is not a within-rule decorrelation failure. Conversely, within a rule the marginals are fixed *exactly* by the design (the within-rule range of `log m̄` is `2.2e−16`, machine precision), so `log m̄` is constant within rule and its within-rule correlation is undefined. `β_m` is therefore **exactly collinear** with the rule intercept `a_r`; it cannot be identified from these designs. This is stronger than the original review's "approximately constant" and "attenuated" wording.

**Recommended fix.**

1. **Pre-register the level of the gate.** If the claim is "at fixed marginals, lift varies independently," the gate should be on the **within-rule** variation of `λ` and on the conditioning of the design matrix, not on pooled rows. If the claim is "the design as a whole is decorrelated," then pooled is the wrong statistic because it confounds between-rule heterogeneity.
2. **Report both levels**, with a variance decomposition: how much of the lift variance is within-rule vs between-rule? If most is between-rule, the design cannot identify `β_λ` separately from template effects.
3. **Compute the VIF on the actual model matrix** (including the random-effect structure or the fixed-effect design), not on the raw row table. A VIF of 11.6 on pooled rows may overstate or understate the collinearity of the estimated `β_λ`.
4. **Add a power calculation for `β_λ` and `β_λR`** based on the design's within-rule lift range and the pilot variance components. This is more informative than a correlation gate.

## 4. Statistical plan and identification

The proposal's Section 11 is thoughtful—crossed random effects, Holm correction, pre-registered equivalence margin, floor/ceiling exclusions, simulation-based power—but the model does not match the variation the design can produce.

### 4.1 Within-rule collinearity of `λ` and `log m̄`

The primary model is:

```
logit p_{r,k} = a_r + β_λ λ_{r,k} + β_{λR} (λ_{r,k} × K_rel,r)
              + β_m log m̄_{r,k} + β_b log b̄_{r,k}
              + θ·(K_attr, K_rel) + u_r + v_k + w_{rk}.
```

Within a rule, the design fixes the singleton marginals exactly. Therefore `log m̄_{r,k}` is constant across designs (empirical within-rule range `2.2e−16`), and `a_r` absorbs it. `β_m` is **exactly collinear with the rule intercept** and is not identified. The original review described this as an errors-in-variables attenuation; the verified statement is stronger: the regressor has zero within-rule variation by construction. Either drop `β_m` or deliberately vary the marginals within rule.

Similarly, `λ_{r,k}` varies over a narrow range in the current sample (median `0.0955` log units, exact LP width `1.172`). `a_r` absorbs its mean; `β_λ` is identified from the within-rule slope over that narrow realized range. `β_λR` is a cross-level interaction: it needs rules with different `K_rel` to have different *slopes*, but the slope is estimated from a narrow range and the `K_rel` main effect is confounded with cell-level differences in the lift level. About `98.7%` of the observed lift variance is between-rule, so the current sample cannot separate the interaction from template effects.

**Fix.** Use a hierarchical measurement-error model:

- treat the estimated `λ` and `log m̄` as noisy measurements with known Monte Carlo standard errors;
- put a prior on the true values, or use SIMEX / multiple imputation;
- use a non-centered parameterization for `u_r`, `v_k`, `w_{rk}`;
- report the posterior/CI for `β_λ`, `β_λR`, and `β_m` under both the naive and measurement-error models.

### 4.2 Binomial vs beta-binomial

The proposal says "binomial GLMM" but also "run enters as a random effect" and "template-by-run random effects." With 57 rules × 20 designs, the binomial likelihood will be overdispersed because generated images within a run are correlated (same model, same weights) and because the checker has rule-specific error. A beta-binomial or a binomial model with an observation-level random effect is more appropriate. The proposal's note that "generated images from one model are never treated as independent replicates" is correct, but the likelihood should reflect it explicitly.

**Fix.** Fit a beta-binomial GLMM (or a binomial model with an observation-level random effect), and report the overdispersion parameter. Use run-level aggregation for the primary analysis and image-level data for the checker/measurement model.

### 4.3 The equivalence margin `δ`

The proposal says `δ` is "fixed from the pilot before confirmatory runs," but there is no definition of how it is chosen, no power analysis for equivalence, and no discussion of what a scientifically meaningful `δ` is on the log or logit scale. For H1, "Γ lies inside δ in all K_rel cells" is an equivalence test; for H2, "the slope's CI lies inside ±δ" is also an equivalence test. These are different `δ` units (log-odds vs slope), so a single `δ` is ambiguous.

**Fix.** Define `δ` separately for each estimand, justify it in units of the outcome (e.g., a 0.1 log-unit composition gap, or a 0.05 change in RSR), and simulate power for the equivalence test. Pre-register the smallest effect of interest, not just the null.

### 4.4 Exclusion rules and selection

The proposal excludes rules with reference RSR below 0.05 or above 0.95, fixed from pilot reference runs. This is sensible for floor/ceiling, but it can induce selection: rules that are hard because of exposure (the very phenomenon under study) are more likely to be excluded, biasing the remaining sample toward easy rules. It also changes the target of inference from "all rules" to "rules with mid-range reference RSR."

**Fix.** Report the excluded set and its exposure distribution. Use a hurdle or zero-one-inflated beta model to include floor/ceiling rules rather than dropping them. Pre-register the exclusion rule and show that the conclusions are robust to including/excluding.

### 4.5 H3, H4, H5, H6

- **H3 (leave-out probes).** The proposal distinguishes "rule removed" from "pairs removed." The implementation does not exist. The design must set `n_r = 0` or zero the pair exposures while holding the rest of the design fixed; this is a constrained LP, and feasibility is not guaranteed. The two leave-out types also differ in many ways (rule exposure, pair exposure, object count), so a simple equality test is underpowered. Use a factorial leave-out design with matched exposure and a pre-registered contrast.
- **H4 (breadth).** Breadth `b_p = exp(H_p)` is defined as the entropy of the co-occurring predicate distribution. The current `design-diagnostics` omits breadth. Before H4 can be tested, implement breadth, estimate it with uncertainty, and check that it is not collinear with frequency and lift. The template balance audit is a start, but it counts vocabulary presence, not predicate frequency.
- **H5 (incremental validity).** The transfer features are interesting, but the proposal's cross-validation is leave-one-run-out, and the transfer matrix is computed from the same checkpoints used to fit the base model. This is a double-dipping risk. Use nested CV: fit the base model on training runs, compute transfer features on held-out runs, and evaluate on a separate held-out set. Pre-register the incremental-validity margin.
- **H6 (region/spectral).** The proposal's region and spectral analyses are well motivated, but they are exploratory and multiplicity-heavy. Pre-register the bands, regions, and normalization; use permutation tests over scenes/rules; report effect sizes with CIs, not just p-values.

### 4.6 The unit of analysis and the "same model" problem

Rules within a run share one model and one budget. The proposal handles this with run random effects, which is right. But the exposure design also couples rules: changing the weight on rule `r` changes the marginal exposure of every predicate that appears in `r`, and therefore the training signal for other rules. The `λ` for rule `r` is not an isolated treatment; it is a function of the whole weight vector. This is a "network interference" problem. The run random effect does not solve it; it only accounts for correlation.

**Fix.** Define the treatment as the **weight vector** `w`, not the scalar `λ_r`. Use a design that varies `w` in a controlled way (e.g., a simplex design or a factorial perturbation of a reference design), and estimate the effect of `w` on the vector of rule outcomes. Alternatively, use a "single-rule perturbation" design: start from a reference `w`, increase/decrease the weight on one rule, and renormalize the rest. This gives a cleaner dose-response and a causal contrast.

## 5. Code review by module

### 5.1 `exposure/rules.py`

**Correct.** Existential quantification over distinct objects, shared bindings within a rule/subset, ties do not satisfy relations. The permutation enumeration is exponential in the number of variables, but the prototype caps at three variables, so it is fine.

**Risks.**

- `satisfies` raises on an empty predicate set; `load_rules` now also rejects empty/duplicate/contradictory/cyclic rules through `validate_rule` before they reach the sampler.
- The relation semantics are marginal-tautological over three objects (see 2.3). This is a benchmark design issue, not a code bug, but the code makes it easy to miss.
- No support for negative predicates, counting, or distance thresholds. If H6/H4 need them, the grammar must be extended.

### 5.2 `exposure/render.py`

**Correct.** Deterministic seeds, 3×3 centers with jitter, anti-aliased 64×64 output, conditioned rejection sampling with a bounded attempt budget, annotations returned alongside the image.

**Risks.**

- The 3×3 grid with jitter makes `left_of`/`above` nearly always true. This is the root of the relation tautology.
- The base distribution is independent across objects and attributes. In the current uniform mixture this makes the independence product an upper bound on the observed lifts (all ≤ 0), rather than a central null.
- No textures, no distractor objects, no variable object count, no occlusion. The proposal mentions texture as optional; the current vocabulary is 3 shapes × 4 colors.
- Rejection sampling can be slow or infeasible for rare rules; `max_attempts` aborts, which is correct, but the aborted condition is not counted. The proposal's "some rules may be too rare" caveat is real.
- The renderer uses a fixed 64×64 image; the proposal's 96×96 fallback is not implemented. Textures and small relations may need it.

### 5.3 `exposure/measure.py`

**Correct.** Named rules, per-condition accepted scenes, all-rule counts, optional singleton/pair subsets, Wilson intervals, deterministic seeds. The diagonal special-case `[1.0, 1.0]` is a deterministic identity (the condition's own rule is satisfied by construction), not a binomial estimate; the code comments should say so explicitly.

**Risks.**

- 100 scenes/condition is far too few for the design LP. The Monte Carlo error dominates the within-rule lift range.
- Wilson intervals are pointwise and not propagated to the mixture/design. The `design.py` caveat acknowledges this, but the design gate does not.
- The pure-Python loop is slow (~0.5–2.5k scenes/s depending on subset measurement; the pilot's subset mode is ~0.6k scenes/s). Vectorize or evaluate annotations directly.
- No deduplication/contradiction check in `load_rules` — **fixed**; `validate_rule` is now applied by `parse_rule`.
- The diagonal `[1,1]` interval can mislead downstream consumers into treating the A-matrix as exact. Document it as an identity.

### 5.4 `exposure/design.py`

**Correct.** Validates the report, builds the constraint matrix, solves a continuous simplex LP with HiGHS, reports infeasible vs error, optionally refines with min-KL. The distinction between infeasible and solver failure is good.

**Risks.**

- The LP treats the estimated A-matrix as exact. No uncertainty propagation.
- Min-KL is not the research objective; it can produce a design with poor power for `β_λ`/`β_λR`.
- SLSQP starts from the LP vertex; the KL gradient is unbounded at zero (floored at 1e-15). This is numerically fragile.
- The `min_weight` floor is a hard constraint; if it is too high, the polytope can be infeasible even when a slightly lower floor would work. Report the minimum feasible floor.
- No KKT check or optimality certificate for the min-KL solution.
- The constraints are equalities for fixed marginals; with estimated marginals, equality is too strong. Use tolerance bands or chance constraints.

### 5.5 `exposure/design_sample.py`

**Correct.** Hit-and-run in the equality nullspace, floor constraints, seeded RNG, strict numerical checks, no clipping/renormalization.

**Risks.**

- No convergence diagnostics (R-hat, ESS, autocorrelation). Burn-in 100 and thin 10 are arbitrary. `exposure.design_chains` now provides multi-chain split-R-hat and autocorrelation ESS, but the default CLI still uses the single-chain sampler.
- A single chain; no multi-chain comparison in the default path (the new `design_chains` module adds one).
- The nullspace is computed from the estimated matrix; uncertainty in the matrix is ignored.
- The sampler only explores the equality-constrained subspace; it does not enforce inequality constraints on lifts or pair exposures.
- The observed within-rule lift range is tiny; the sampler may be exploring a genuinely narrow polytope, not just under-mixing. Both need to be diagnosed. `exposure.design_bounds` now computes the exact LP width, which distinguishes the two: the polytope is wider (`1.172` median) than the realized sample (`0.0955`).

### 5.6 `exposure/design_diagnostics.py` and `design_strata.py`

**Correct.** Pooled correlations/VIF, per-stratum correlations, explicit caveats, no false gate claim. The strata analysis correctly identifies that within-rule correlation can be undefined.

**Risks.**

- Pooled correlations are the wrong level for the proposal's gate (see 3.4).
- VIF is computed on pooled rows, not on the model matrix.
- No uncertainty on the correlations; no bootstrap.
- Breadth is omitted.
- The `gate_status: not_assessed` is honest but leaves the Stage 0 decision ambiguous. Add an explicit "fails the proposal's stated gate" field, with the reason.

### 5.7 `exposure/templates.py`

**Correct.** Deterministic 57-template generator, grammar feasibility witnesses, explicit limitations, manifest with counts.

**Risks.**

- Hand-designed bundles, not a factorial/counterbalanced design (see 3.1).
- Relation kind confounded with attribute type.
- No held-out split.
- Duplicate/contradictory predicates not rejected by the loader — **fixed** in `validate_rule`.
- The "extra triangle" template fixes vocabulary coverage but distorts cell balance.
- No texture, no counting, no negative predicates.

### 5.8 `exposure/grammar_audit.py`

**Correct.** Exact feasibility checks for conflicting attributes and strict order cycles, three-object witnesses, deterministic coordinates. The proof sketch is clear.

**Risks.**

- Feasibility is structural, not sampling feasibility. A rule can be feasible but have vanishing probability under rejection sampling.
- The witness uses a canonical scene, not a random draw from the renderer; it does not establish that the condition is reachable with the actual jitter/separation.
- No check for duplicate predicates or vacuous rules.

### 5.9 `exposure/checker.py`

**Correct.** Deterministic palette/segmentation pipeline, clean-render audit, conservative sharpening fallback, explicit rejection on ambiguous components.

**Risks.**

- Hard-coded geometry (see 3.2).
- Not validated on generated images.
- High rejection under blur/brightness (84.5–90% at blur radius 1.0); per-rule error rates on generated images are unknown. The audit now reports rejection, coverage, and conditional/unconditional sensitivity and specificity per rule, which separates misclassification from attrition.
- The sharpening fallback is a patch, not a general solution.
- The checker's object count/width assumptions encode the renderer, so it cannot be used as an independent measurement instrument.

### 5.10 `exposure/pilot_train.py`

**Correct.** Compact conditional U-Net, DDPM epsilon prediction, EMA, condition dropout, deterministic option, checkpoint schema v3 with RNG state, resume compatibility checks, peak memory/timing, oracle sampler test.

**Risks.**

- Width 16/batch 2 is too small for the task; 32k updates is too few.
- No x0 clipping during sampling.
- No LR schedule, warmup, or gradient clipping.
- One cross-attention block at 16×16; no explicit 2D positional encoding; no object-slot attention.
- No Min-SNR or other loss weighting.
- The null condition is a learned token; classifier-free guidance is implemented, but the guidance sweep is small and the samples are not usable.
- No reference model; no exact-score baseline.
- The checkpoint's `last_loss` is a single batch, not a running estimate; it is not a learning curve.

### 5.11 `exposure/human_audit.py` and `audit_agreement.py`

**Correct.** Blinded preparation, prediction/scoring separation, no fabricated labels, disagreement preservation, explicit denominators. This is a strong design.

**Risks.**

- No generated images to audit.
- The sampling frame for the human audit is not yet pre-registered; the proposal requires complexity stratification and at least 300 images.
- Agreement is descriptive; no latent-class model to estimate checker sensitivity/specificity.

### 5.12 `exposure/mosaic_contrast.py`

**Correct.** Descriptive run-level summary, equal cell weighting, paired-seed contrasts, explicit note that it does not train models or decide the gate.

**Risks.**

- No actual MOSAIC replication; only the analysis plumbing.
- The contrast assumes a 2×2 complexity × imbalance design; the current renderer does not implement MOSAIC's counting/spatial-relation benchmark.
- No power analysis for the replication.

### 5.13 `exposure/gaussian_diagnostic.py`

**Correct.** Analytic Bayes denoiser for a 1D Gaussian mixture, Monte Carlo check, loss normalization.

**Risks.**

- 1D only; does not exercise the image model or the 3-object scene distribution.
- No connection to the actual training loss or the composition gap.

### 5.14 Documentation and packaging

- `README.md` and `docs/architecture.md` say the repo "does not yet train a model," but `pilot_train.py`, `docs/pilot-training.md`, and the 32k-update results exist. This is stale and undermines trust in the docs. Update the top-level description and module table.
- `pyproject.toml` lists only `pillow` and `scipy`; `torch` is optional. This is good for CPU-only use, but the test suite skips 55 tests without it. Add a CI job with torch (CPU) so the training/sampling tests run.

- Outputs are stored as JSON with schema versions; add a top-level manifest with git SHA, command, seed, and environment for each output. This would make the review reproducible.

## 6. Adjacent research and positioning

The proposal's literature review is a good start, but several adjacent papers change the framing. The list below is limited to papers I checked at the abstract level in this session. Claims are paraphrased; verify full texts before submission.

### 6.1 Closest prior work

**MOSAIC — Jeong, Uselis, Laina, Oh, Rohrbach, "When Do Diffusion Models Learn to Generate Multiple Objects?", ICML 2026 (arXiv:2605.00273).**

- Introduces `mosaic` (Multi-Object Spatial relations, AttrIbution, Counting), a controlled framework for multi-object generation.
- Finds that **scene complexity dominates concept imbalance** in low-data regimes, counting is uniquely difficult, and compositional generalization collapses as more combinations are held out.
- **Implication:** MOSAIC already covers multi-object spatial relations, attributes, counting, complexity vs imbalance, and compositional holdouts. The proposal cannot claim novelty for "a synthetic compositional benchmark" or "complexity matters more than imbalance." The differentiator must be the **exposure-level decomposition** and the **marginal-preserving manipulation**. The proposal says this, but the current 57-template set is essentially a smaller MOSAIC-like benchmark, so the novelty claim needs to be sharpened and the MOSAIC replication must actually run.

**Okawa, Lubana, Dick, Tanaka, "Compositional Abilities Emerge Multiplicatively", NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2310.09336).**

- Controlled synthetic diffusion study; compositional performance emerges suddenly due to multiplicative reliance on constituent performance; rare compositions need more optimization steps.
- **Implication:** This is the source of the product null. The proposal's logit gap is a misreading of the multiplicative claim (see 2.1). The correct null is on the probability/log scale, and the "multiplicative" structure should be tested with a log-linear model, not a logit difference. Okawa also predicts that rare compositions need more steps, which is directly relevant to `N50` and to the "time-to-threshold" trajectory analysis.

**Bradley, "Local Mechanisms of Compositional Generalization in Conditional Diffusion", ICML 2026 (arXiv:2509.16447).**

- Studies **length generalization** (more objects than seen in training) in CLEVR; shows that successful models have **local conditional scores** (sparse dependencies on pixels and conditioners); a causal intervention enforcing locality restores length generalization.
- **Implication:** The proposal cites Bradley for "local conditional scores underlie compositional generalization," which is broadly right, but the setting is length generalization, not attribute binding. The causal intervention is a strong template for the proposal's H5/H6: instead of only measuring transfer, **intervene** on the architecture to enforce locality and test whether relational generalization improves. This would turn a correlational diagnostic into a causal one.

**Soiffer, Squires, Guan, Hartford, Ravikumar, "Catastrophic Compositional Generation: Why Vanilla Diffusion Models Fail to Extrapolate", arXiv:2606.23920 (2026).**

- Argues that compositional generation is often **infeasible** for vanilla conditional diffusion models; conjectures that no inference-time technique can efficiently produce the target distribution in certain well-motivated settings; score-estimation error is catastrophic when the target is OOD relative to the sources.
- **Implication:** This is a direct challenge to H3 (zero-shot success on held-out rules requires pairwise support). If the target conditional is OOD enough, even pairwise support may not suffice. The proposal should cite Soiffer as a competing prediction and design a test that distinguishes "pairwise support is sufficient" from "the target is infeasible for this architecture." It also motivates the learnability pre-gate: if vanilla diffusion cannot extrapolate, the study should measure that failure, not assume it away.

**Favero, Sclocchi, Cagnetta, Frossard, Wyart, "How Compositional Generalization and Creativity Improve as Diffusion Models are Trained", ICML 2025 (arXiv:2502.12089).**

- Diffusion models learn compositional rules with sample complexity that scales **polynomially with context size**; higher-level features with longer contexts need more data; intermediate training yields local coherence but not global coherence.
- **Implication:** This gives a theoretical prediction for H1/H2: the required exposure should grow with the **binding/context size** of the rule, not just the number of predicates. The proposal's `K_attr`/`K_rel` counts are crude proxies for context size; a better measure is the number of object variables and the length of the dependency chain. Favero also predicts a **coherence-length** phenomenon that the proposal's region/spectral analysis could test.

**Udandarao et al., "No 'Zero-Shot' Without Exponential Data", NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv:2404.04125).**

- Across 34 models and five pretraining datasets, downstream performance follows a **sample-inefficient log-linear scaling trend**; models need exponentially more data for linear improvements; long-tail performance is poor.
- **Implication:** This is directly relevant to H4 (breadth matters beyond frequency) and to the `N50` framing. It predicts that frequency will dominate and that breadth may have a smaller effect than the proposal hopes. It also provides a scaling-law template: fit `RSR` vs `log n_r` and test whether the slope depends on breadth/complexity. The proposal's `N50` is a threshold version of this; report the full curve, not just `N50`.

**Qu & Xie, "Impact of Pretraining Word Co-occurrence on Compositional Generalization in Multimodal Models", arXiv:2507.08000 (2025).**

- Uses **PMI** to separate pair co-occurrence from single-word frequency; finds a strong correlation between pretraining PMI and zero-shot accuracy in CLIP (r=0.97), reproduced on natural images (r=0.75), and transferred to LMMs.
- **Implication:** This is the closest existing evidence for the proposal's pair-level lift. The proposal's `λ_pq` is essentially a PMI. The novelty is the controlled diffusion setting and the marginal-preserving design, not the PMI idea itself. Cite Qu & Xie explicitly and differentiate: they study pretraining co-occurrence in CLIP/LMMs, not diffusion training or exposure allocation.

**Yu & Gao, "Improving Compositional Generation with Diffusion Models Using Lift Scores", ICML 2025 (arXiv:2505.13740).**

- Introduces **lift scores** as an inference-time resampling criterion for compositional generation; evaluates whether a sample aligns with each condition and composes the results.
- **Implication:** The proposal's "lift" is a data-level log ratio of exposure to independence; Yu & Gao's "lift score" is an inference-time alignment score. The terminology collision will confuse reviewers and search. Rename the proposal's quantity to **exposure lift** or **co-occurrence lift**, and cite Yu & Gao as a different use of "lift."

### 6.2 Mechanism and intervention work

**Mlodozeniec et al., "Influence Functions for Scalable Data Attribution in Diffusion Models", arXiv:2410.13850 (2024/2025).**

- Develops influence functions for diffusion models, with K-FAC approximations, to predict how removing training data changes the probability of generating an example.
- **Implication:** This is a principled alternative/complement to the proposal's counterfactual transfer experiments. Instead of K AdamW steps and a transfer matrix, use influence functions to estimate the effect of removing/upweighting a rule's data. It is cheaper and has a causal interpretation under the linearized model. The proposal's H5 could use influence functions as a baseline or a validation of the transfer features.

**Go et al., "Addressing Negative Transfer in Diffusion Models", NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2306.00354).**

- Frames diffusion training as multi-task learning over noise levels; task affinity diminishes as the noise-level gap widens; negative transfer occurs; interval clustering mitigates it.
- **Implication:** The proposal's "interference" measures should account for the noise-level structure. A rule's training signal is not a single task; it is a set of denoising tasks across timesteps. The transfer matrix `T_{r←q}` should be computed per SNR band (the proposal says this) and compared to the task-affinity baseline. Go et al. also suggest that some "composition costs" may be negative transfer across noise levels, not a property of the composition itself.

**Nützel, Dombrowski, Kainz, "GRASP: Guided Residual Adapters with Sample-wise Partitioning", arXiv:2512.01675 (2025/2026).**

- Long-tail text-to-image flow matching degrades because of low head-vs-tail gradient alignment during fine-tuning; a deterministic partition of the conditioning space with group-specific residual adapters fixes it; the partition is static, so every tail sample updates its expert.
- **Implication:** This is a strong baseline/intervention for the allocation contribution. If the proposal's optimization finds an exposure allocation, GRASP suggests an architectural alternative: partition the conditioning space and give each partition its own adapter. The paper also supports the proposal's focus on gradient conflict, but warns that conditioning- and sampling-side interventions may not address the optimization pathology.

**Park et al., "Rare-to-Frequent: Unlocking Compositional Generation Power of Diffusion Models on Rare Concepts with LLM Guidance", ICLR 2025 (arXiv:2410.22376).**

- Training-free LLM-guided sampling that exposes frequent concepts relevant to a rare target during diffusion sampling; improves rare-composition alignment.
- **Implication:** This is an inference-time baseline for the proposal's "exposure" intervention. If R2F can improve rare compositions without changing training exposure, then the allocation contribution must show a training-time advantage over inference-time guidance at equal compute.

**Yang et al., "Swing-by Dynamics in Concept Learning and Compositional Generalization", arXiv:2410.08309 (2024/2025).**

- Proposes a structured identity mapping (SIM) task on a Gaussian mixture; analyzes learning dynamics; explains sequential generalization and non-monotonic test loss in early training.
- **Implication:** This is a theory template for the proposal's trajectories. The SIM task is a simplified version of the proposal's synthetic benchmark; it could provide closed-form predictions for `N50` and for the order in which rules are learned. The proposal's "time-to-threshold" analysis should be compared to SIM's predictions.

### 6.3 What the literature implies for novelty

The proposal's candidate contributions are:

1. marginal-preserving exposure manipulation;
2. an independence baseline and composition gap;
3. exposure thresholds (`N50`) rather than accuracy at one budget;
4. interference measures with causal validation;
5. exposure allocation as constrained optimization.

Against the literature:

- **(1) is the strongest differentiator.** Qu & Xie use PMI but do not manipulate exposure; MOSAIC varies complexity/imbalance but not marginal-preserving exposure; Okawa varies frequency but not the predicate/pair/rule decomposition. A controlled, marginal-preserving exposure manipulation in diffusion training is genuinely novel if executed well.
- **(2) is weak as stated** because the product null is a heuristic and the logit gap is wrong. A binding-aware log-linear null would be a real contribution, but it needs to be developed, not assumed.
- **(3) is a useful framing** but `N50` is a threshold on a curve; report the full dose-response and the threshold. Udandarao's log-linear scaling is the natural comparison.
- **(4) is ambitious** and may be the most interesting part, but it requires a model that learns and a causal design. Influence functions (Mlodozeniec) and local-score interventions (Bradley) are stronger causal tools than the current transfer matrix.
- **(5) is conditional** on (2) or (4) holding, and it needs a design that actually varies the dose. As written, the optimization is a solution looking for an identified surrogate.

**Bottom line:** the paper is currently a benchmark/instrument paper with a promising exposure-decomposition idea. To become the proposed causal/optimization paper, it needs (a) a learnable model, (b) a valid null, (c) a two-sided dose, and (d) a design with power for the interaction. Without those, the honest contribution is a negative/methods result: "we built the instrument, and vanilla diffusion did not learn the benchmark." That is publishable if framed correctly, but it is not the paper the proposal describes.

## 7. Prioritized recommendations

### P0 — before any more exposure experiments

1. **Pre-register the composition-gap scale and a binding-aware null.** Both `Γ^logit = logit(RSR) − logit(Π c_p)` and `Γ^log = log RSR − Σ log c_p` are zero under the multiplicative null; choose one as primary, justify it in outcome units, and report the other as a sensitivity. Add a null simulation that verifies both are zero. Do not repeat the original review's `Γ^logit` table, which evaluated a different expression. Better, pre-register a log-linear/maximum-entropy null with lower-order marginals and define the gap as the high-order interaction.
2. **Fix the relation predicates.** Bind relations to attributed objects, add graded/negative relations, and ensure singleton relation exposure is in a nontrivial range (e.g., [0.2, 0.8]). Report relation exposure conditional on the attribute bundle. Redesign the `K_rel` cells so relation count is not confounded with object count.
3. **Add a learnability pre-gate and do not proceed until it passes.** Overfit 1/10/100 scenes, then the full template set; require median RSR ≥ 0.3. If it fails, report the failure as the result and stop.
4. **Make the dose two-sided and wide.** Build a factorial template set with coupled and anti-coupled components; use a D-optimal/c-optimal design for `β_λ` and `β_λR`; report the attainable within-rule lift range per rule; drop rules with range < 0.5 log units.
5. **Revise the compute plan.** Measure throughput and time-to-RSR for a capacity sweep; update the 200–250 GPU-hour estimate. A 10× contingency is realistic.

### P1 — before confirmatory runs

6. **Counterbalance the templates.** Full factorial crossing, held-out split, balance audit on actual predicate frequencies, duplicate/contradiction rejection.
7. **Replace/augment the checker.** Robust segmentation, per-predicate sensitivity/specificity on generated images, latent-class correction for RSR.
8. **Vectorize the A-matrix and propagate uncertainty.** 10^4–10^5 scenes/template, bootstrap/Bayesian intervals, chance-constrained/robust LP, convex KL or interior-point start.
9. **Fix the design diagnostics.** Evaluate the gate within-rule and on the model matrix; report variance decomposition and power for `β_λ`/`β_λR`; add ESS/R-hat to hit-and-run.
10. **Upgrade the statistical model.** Beta-binomial/hierarchical likelihood, measurement-error model for `λ` and `log m̄`, non-centered random effects, pre-registered `δ` per estimand, hurdle model for floor/ceiling.
11. **Pre-register the analysis.** Exact contrasts, exclusion rules, primary/secondary outcomes, multiplicity correction, and the scale of `Γ`.
12. **Run the MOSAIC replication.** The proposal's Stage 0 gate requires it; it is currently unexecuted.

### P2 — engineering and reproducibility

13. **Update `README.md` and `docs/architecture.md`** to reflect the training modules and the current Stage 0 status.
14. **Add CI with torch (CPU)** so the 55 skipped tests run; add `ruff`/`mypy`.
15. **Add a manifest** with git SHA, command, seed, environment, and output hashes for every saved JSON.
16. **Add property-based tests** for `satisfies` (permutation invariance, binding, contradiction), design LP (brute-force small cases, KKT), and the statistical model (recover known parameters from simulated data).
17. **Add a `--plan`/`--estimate` mode** that reports scenes/s, GPU-hours, and expected RSR before launching a long run.

## 8. Cheap, decisive experiments

These are ordered by information per unit cost. They do not require the full confirmatory budget.

**E0 — Null simulation for `Γ` (hours, CPU).** Simulate a perfectly multiplicative model with `c` in {0.5, 0.7, 0.8, 0.9} and `k` in {1,2,3}; verify that both `Γ^logit` and `Γ^log` are zero under `RSR = Π c_p`, and quantify floating-point and boundary behavior. This is the single most important sanity check and can be done in a notebook. A regression test now covers it in `tests/test_composition.py`.

**E1 — Binding-aware null on the existing A-matrix (hours, CPU).** Fit a log-linear model to the 57-condition matrix with singleton and pair terms; compute the high-order interaction for each rule. Compare the ranking to the current lift. This tests whether the product null and the log-linear null disagree on which rules are "hard."

**E2 — Learnability gate (1–2 GPU-days).** Overfit 1, 10, 100 scenes with width 64, batch 32, x0 clipping, cosine LR. If the model cannot overfit 10 scenes, the architecture/training is broken; if it can, proceed to the full template set. This is the critical-path experiment.

**E3 — Single-rule dose-response (1–2 GPU-days).** Pick one attribute-only rule and one relation rule with a wide achievable lift range. Train 5–7 designs × 3 seeds at a short budget. Estimate the slope and `N50`. This tests the measurement pipeline end-to-end before the full 60-run experiment.

**E4 — Relation redesign (hours, CPU).** Implement attribute-bound relations and measure singleton exposure; compare to the current existential relation. If the new relation exposure is in [0.2, 0.8], the relational contrast becomes meaningful.

**E5 — Template balance audit (hours, CPU).** Build the full factorial template set and compute the color × shape × relation × cell contingency table. Report standardized residuals. This will show whether the current confounds are fixed.

**E6 — Checker on generated images (1 GPU-day + human hours).** Sample 300 images from the best available checkpoint, run the checker and a blinded human audit, and estimate per-rule sensitivity/specificity. If the checker error is > 2%, fix it before any RSR analysis.

**E7 — Vectorized A-matrix (hours, CPU).** Reimplement `measure.estimate` with NumPy/annotation-level evaluation; verify against the existing loop on a small sample; measure throughput. This unlocks 10^5/template.

**E8 — MOSAIC replication (2–5 GPU-days).** Implement the 2×2 complexity × imbalance contrast on the current renderer or a MOSAIC-compatible one. The proposal's Stage 0 gate requires it; do it before the confirmatory exposure runs.

## 9. What to cut or reframe

The proposal is ambitious: six hypotheses, a benchmark, a causal diagnostic program, an optimization contribution, and a MOSAIC replication. The current evidence supports none of the causal/optimization claims yet. A credible path is to **cut scope in stages**:

- **Stage A (instrument + negative result).** Fix the null, redesign relations, build the factorial benchmark, run the learnability gate, and report whether vanilla diffusion learns the benchmark. This is a methods/benchmark paper with a strong negative result if the model fails. It is publishable and honest.
- **Stage B (measurement).** If the model learns, run the single-rule dose-response and the full exposure decomposition. Report `N50`, the log-linear gap, and the within-rule slopes. This is the core empirical paper.
- **Stage C (causal/optimization).** Only if Stage B identifies a robust `β_λ`/`β_λR`: add the transfer/influence diagnostics, the conditioning arms, and the allocation optimization. Do not promise this in the abstract until Stage B succeeds.

The proposal's "optimization contribution is conditional on H2 or H5 holding" is the right instinct. Make the conditionality explicit in the paper's claims and in the compute plan.

## Appendix D: One-paragraph summary for a collaborator

The codebase is a clean, honest instrument-development prototype: rules, renderer, measurement, design LP, diagnostics, checker, and training scaffolding are tested and documented. The research design is not yet measurable. The original review's central algebra claim was wrong: the stated `Γ = logit(RSR) − logit(Π c_p)` is exactly zero under the multiplicative null, and the reported `−2.20/−4.11` values belong to a different expression. That correction does not rescue the design. Relation predicates are near-tautologies (`≈0.9993` singleton exposure) under existential semantics, so the relational contrast is confounded; relation kind is also deterministically confounded with endpoint binding and only `3/12` color–shape pairs are co-bound to one object. The sampled exposure dose is narrow (`0.0955` log units within rule, `98.7%` between-rule), while the exact LP polytope is wider (`1.172` median, `23/57` rules admit positive lift) and no sampled chain reaches the positive corner. The decorrelation gate fails by the proposal's own criterion (pooled `r = −0.446`, VIF `11.6`), the pooled statistic is a between-rule artifact, and `β_m` is exactly collinear with the rule intercept. The checker is an inverse renderer whose errors are attrition (84.5–90% rejection at blur radius 1.0), not misclassification. The model has not learned the benchmark (12k samples were noise; 32k yielded two inspected samples, one red shape and no clear relation), and there is no learnability gate. The highest-value adjacent work is MOSAIC (which already covers the synthetic multi-object benchmark), Okawa (the multiplicative null), Favero (context-size sample complexity), Soiffer (OOD infeasibility), Bradley (local-score intervention), and Qu & Xie (PMI). The path forward is: fix the null's scale and binding, redesign relations and templates, add a learnability gate, target the design at `β_λ`/`β_λR` with a sampler that reaches the feasible dose, propagate uncertainty, and only then run the confirmatory exposure experiment. Until then, the honest deliverable is a methods/negative result, not the causal/optimization paper the proposal describes.
